# Relationship evidence guide

Six sub-dimensions. Same shape per entry as `pdf-sdlc-health/references/sdlc-evidence-guide.md`: definition, canonical question, evidence to look for, warning signs, RAG criteria.

## Provenance

The first four sub-dimensions adapt the trust equation in David Maister, Charles Green and Robert Galford, *The Trusted Advisor* (2000): trustworthiness rises with credibility, reliability and intimacy, and falls as self-orientation rises. "Intimacy" is rendered here as **Candour** (the safety to raise hard things) and self-orientation is inverted into **Client-Centred Orientation** so that GREEN means good across every row. Engagement Cadence and Alignment are added because a delivery relationship also lives or dies on whether people actually meet and whether they want the same thing. No model here is vendor-specific.

---

## Credibility

**Definition.** Whether the stakeholder trusts our competence: that we know our domain, tell the truth, and give accurate read-outs.

**Canonical question:** *"Does this stakeholder act as though they believe what we tell them and rate our expertise, or do they second-guess, re-check, or seek parallel opinions?"*

**Evidence to look for:**
- They accept our estimates and assessments without demanding independent verification each time.
- They bring us problems beyond the immediate task because they value the judgement.
- Our status read-outs are taken at face value.

**Warning signs:**
- Every assessment is re-checked or routed past someone else.
- Our competence is questioned in front of others.
- They ask the same question of multiple people hoping for a different answer.

**RAG criteria:**
- **Green:** Our word is trusted; expertise is sought; little second-guessing.
- **Amber:** Credibility is conditional or uneven across topics; some re-checking.
- **Red:** Routine second-guessing; expertise openly doubted; parallel opinions sought as a matter of course.

## Reliability

**Definition.** Whether we do what we say, when we said, repeatedly. The track record on commitments.

**Canonical question:** *"Over the last month, did we keep the commitments we made to this stakeholder — actions, dates, follow-ups — and do they expect us to?"*

**Evidence to look for:**
- Action items closed by the dates given, or renegotiated before they slipped.
- Follow-ups arrive without chasing.
- Meetings are honoured or rescheduled, not dropped.

**Warning signs:**
- Commitments quietly missed and not mentioned.
- The stakeholder has started chasing routine items.
- "I'll get back to you" rarely converts to a reply.

**RAG criteria:**
- **Green:** Commitments consistently met or renegotiated transparently; no chasing needed.
- **Amber:** Mostly reliable but with slips that are not always flagged.
- **Red:** Commitments routinely missed; the stakeholder no longer assumes follow-through.

## Candour

**Definition.** Whether hard things can be raised in both directions early, without the relationship absorbing damage. The safety to deliver bad news.

**Canonical question:** *"When something is going wrong, do we hear it from them early and do they hear it from us early, or does bad news arrive late and by surprise?"*

**Evidence to look for:**
- Bad news is shared before the next scheduled forum, not sprung in it.
- The stakeholder tells us about internal problems, politics, or shifting priorities.
- Disagreement is voiced and worked through rather than suppressed.

**Warning signs:**
- We learn of their dissatisfaction from a third party.
- Issues surface for the first time in a steering meeting.
- Surface politeness with no real problems ever named.

**RAG criteria:**
- **Green:** Two-way early candour; hard topics handled as normal business.
- **Amber:** Candour one-directional, or hard topics handled but late.
- **Red:** Bad news arrives by surprise; real concerns are routed around us.

## Client-Centred Orientation

**Definition.** Whether we are seen to act in the stakeholder's interest and the engagement's, rather than chiefly our own. (The inverse of self-orientation.)

**Canonical question:** *"Does this stakeholder behave as though they believe we have their interests in mind — or as though they think we are mainly protecting scope, margin, or our own position?"*

**Evidence to look for:**
- We offer value beyond the contracted scope when it helps them (a suggestion, a heads-up, a small improvement).
- We name trade-offs honestly, including ones that cost us.
- The contract is a shared reference, not a shield we hide behind.

**Warning signs:**
- The SoW or proposal is invoked defensively in routine discussion.
- Every request is met first with a change-request reflex.
- The stakeholder implies we optimise for billing over outcome.

**RAG criteria:**
- **Green:** Seen as partners acting in their interest; trade-offs named honestly.
- **Amber:** Generally constructive but occasionally seen as protecting our own position.
- **Red:** Seen as self-interested; the contract is weaponised in both directions.

## Engagement Cadence

**Definition.** Whether the rhythm of contact actually happens and is valued: 1:1s, reviews, responsiveness.

**Canonical question:** *"Are the planned touchpoints with this stakeholder happening as intended, attended, and treated as worthwhile — and do they respond to us in good time?"*

**Evidence to look for:**
- 1:1s and reviews happen on cadence and are rarely cancelled.
- The stakeholder turns up, prepared, and engaged.
- Responses to our messages arrive within a sensible window.

**Warning signs:**
- 1:1s repeatedly cancelled, shortened, or delegated away.
- Reviews trimmed to cut interaction time.
- Messages left unanswered or acknowledged only after chasing.

**RAG criteria:**
- **Green:** Cadence honoured; touchpoints valued; timely responses.
- **Amber:** Cadence slipping; some cancellations; responsiveness variable.
- **Red:** Cadence collapsing; the stakeholder is withdrawing contact.

---

## Alignment

**Definition.** Whether we and the stakeholder want the same thing: shared understanding of scope, priorities, and what success looks like.

**Canonical question:** *"If I asked this stakeholder to describe the current priorities and the definition of success, would it match ours — and has that match held since the last check?"*

**Evidence to look for:**
- Their stated priorities match the charter success criteria and the current plan.
- Scope expectations are shared; no quiet divergence.
- Goals are stable between conversations, or changes are surfaced and agreed.

**Warning signs:**
- Priorities shift between conversations without notice.
- Their definition of "done" or "on track" differs from ours.
- A milestone review reveals a scope expectation we did not know about.

**RAG criteria:**
- **Green:** Shared, stable understanding of scope, priorities, and success.
- **Amber:** Broad alignment with drift at the edges; needs periodic re-confirmation.
- **Red:** Material divergence on scope or priorities; surprises at review points.

---

## Overall RAG rule

Same as the other health cards: overall = worst of sub-dimensions unless the user explicitly justifies a higher rating, with the justification recorded. A worsening trend across two consecutive cards is a finding in its own right, even when no single dimension is RED.
