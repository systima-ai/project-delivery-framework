# Diagnose situation workflow

A standalone, stage-agnostic utility (a sibling of `pdf-elicit` and `pdf-red-team`). It takes a described delivery situation, recognises the pattern, and turns it into an owned action plan that routes to the workflows that do the actual work.

Produces `_pdf-output/engagements/{active}/diagnostics/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>.md`.

**Method (public).** The response spine is generic structured problem-solving: understand before acting (frame the problem in the stakeholder's terms), generate options, recommend with a named trade-off, plan, then communicate. It echoes the define-measure-analyse loop of A3/PDCA and the "seek first to understand" discipline; none of it is vendor-specific. The situation patterns are common delivery archetypes, described generically in `references/situation-patterns.md`.

## Preconditions

- Active engagement. The diagnostic reads the charter, RAID, and (if present) the stakeholder map and latest health cards for context.

## Intent: diagnose (default)

1. **Hear the situation.** *"Describe what's happening in a few sentences. What changed, who's involved, and why it's a problem now."*
2. **Match a pattern.** Read `references/situation-patterns.md` and identify the closest archetype (or "general dissatisfaction" when none fits cleanly). Name the match and its signature back to the user; confirm or re-match. More than one pattern can apply.
3. **Clarify.** Ask only the questions the matched pattern needs (the pattern lists them). Pull what you can from the charter, RAID, and stakeholder map first so you do not ask what is already known.
4. **Frame the problem.** State it in one sentence, in the affected stakeholder's terms, with the impact and the consequence of inaction. This is the framing the response is built on.
5. **Generate options.** At least two, one of which is "hold / do not act yet" where that is genuine. For each: what it is, what it costs, what it risks.
6. **Recommend.** Name one option and the trade-off being accepted. Honest, not self-serving for the delivery team.
7. **Plan.** Concrete next actions, each with an owner and a date. Include any contingency the pattern calls for.
8. **Communicate.** State who needs to hear what, in what order, and through which channel; flag where a stakeholder must hear it from us before they hear it elsewhere.
9. **Route.** Point each action at the PDF workflow that executes it (the pattern names the usual routes). Examples: an out-of-contract ask routes to `pdf-create-change-request`; a decision needing sign-off routes to `pdf-decide-escalation` / `pdf-write-escalation-memo`; a relationship signal routes to `pdf-relationship-health`; a staffing constraint routes to `pdf-create-capacity-plan`.
10. **Compose:**

```markdown
---
artifact_type: situation-diagnostic
engagement: <slug>
date: <YYYY-MM-DD>
slug: <slug>
pattern: <matched pattern name(s)>
generated_by: pdf-diagnose-situation
routes_to: [<skill>, ...]
---

# Situation diagnostic — <short title> — <date>

## Situation

<2–4 sentences as described, plus context pulled from charter/RAID/map.>

## Pattern

<matched pattern and its signature; why it fits.>

## Problem framed

<one sentence in the affected stakeholder's terms: the problem, the impact, the cost of inaction.>

## Options

### Option A — <name>
- What it is: <one line>
- Cost: <one line>
- Risk: <one line>

### Option B — <name>
(Same fields.)

### Option C — Hold / do not act yet
- Implication: <one line>

## Recommendation

<one paragraph. Names the option. States the trade-off.>

## Action plan

| Action | Owner | By when | Routes to |
|--------|-------|---------|-----------|
| <action> | <name> | <date> | <pdf-skill or —> |

## Communication

<who hears what, in what order, through which channel; what must reach a stakeholder from us first.>

## Cross-references

- Related risks: <R-NNN or "none">
- Related change requests / decisions: <IDs or "none">

---

_Generated by `pdf-diagnose-situation`._
```

11. **Hand off.** Offer to launch the first routed workflow immediately.

## Intent: validate

- [ ] A pattern is named (or "general" with a reason).
- [ ] The problem is framed in the stakeholder's terms with impact and cost-of-inaction.
- [ ] At least two options, one of which is a genuine "hold" where applicable.
- [ ] Recommendation names an option and a trade-off.
- [ ] Every action has an owner and a date.
- [ ] Routed workflows exist in the framework.

## Intent: dump-merge

Accept a dumped account (an email thread, a call transcript, a vented Slack message). Extract the situation, match the pattern, and propose the diagnostic for confirmation before writing.

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- **Jumping to a solution before framing the problem.** The frame comes first; an unframed fix usually solves the wrong thing.
- **A single-option diagnostic.** If there is only one option, it is a task, not a decision; route it directly to its workflow instead.
- **A recommendation with no trade-off.** Every real choice gives something up; name it.
- **Inventing a crisis.** If the situation is routine, say so and route to the ordinary workflow rather than dramatising it.
- **Acting where the real need is escalation.** If the decision sits above the delivery line, route to `pdf-decide-escalation`; do not absorb a decision that is not yours.

## Red-team posture

Off. Internal decision-support. Any client-facing message that results carries its own red-team via the workflow that produces it (`pdf-write-escalation-memo`, `pdf-write-stakeholder-update`, `pdf-create-change-request`).
