# Situation patterns

Common delivery situations, described generically. Each entry gives the signature, the questions to clarify, the shape of the response, and the PDF workflows it usually routes to. These are archetypes for recognition, not scripts; adapt to the specifics in front of you. None is specific to any firm.

The response spine is the same throughout: **Clarify → Frame → Options → Recommend → Plan → Communicate**, then route.

---

## P1 — Ambiguous opportunity

**Signature.** An early or pre-engagement ask that is too generic to act on safely ("move our solution to the cloud", "add some AI"). The risk is committing to a shape before the problem is understood.

**Clarify.**
- What outcome does the client actually want, in their words?
- Who is the real audience and decision-maker for this?
- What is known about the client's context, constraints, and history?

**Response shape.** Resist sizing until the ask is framed. Divide research across the team. Establish the client's hierarchy and the individual motivations. Present back a framed understanding before a proposal; ask for a steer.

**Routes to.** `pdf-qualify-opportunity`, `pdf-shape-opportunity`, `pdf-map-stakeholders`.

---

## P2 — Health drifting (green to amber)

**Signature.** Delivery still reports on track, but a relationship or visibility signal is off: a senior stakeholder says they lack status, 1:1s are slipping, tone has cooled. The risk is a quiet slide that nobody has named.

**Clarify.**
- What specifically changed, and when?
- Which stakeholder is affected, and where do they sit on the map?
- Is this a reporting gap, an alignment gap, or a relationship gap?

**Response shape.** Re-check the stakeholder map for a missed or newly-important stakeholder (often an organisational change on the client side). Adjust the reporting cadence or template to the level that stakeholder needs. Baseline the relationship so the drift is measured, not just felt.

**Routes to.** `pdf-relationship-health`, `pdf-map-stakeholders`, `pdf-create-comms-plan`, `pdf-update-raid`.

---

## P3 — Expectation gap surfaced late (amber to red)

**Signature.** The team is delivering to the original agreement and reporting "on track", but a milestone review reveals the client expected something different in scope or priority. The risk is that "on track" was measured against the wrong baseline.

**Clarify.**
- What did the client expect that we did not deliver, and where did the expectation come from?
- Where did our understanding and theirs diverge, and when?
- Was the divergence visible earlier in any signal we missed?

**Response shape.** Clarify the real expectations directly. Propose a plan to deliver against them, with risks and assumptions stated. Get the plan approved in writing. Then fix the reporting that let the gap hide: walk stakeholders through the report, increase frequency or detail, and confirm shared understanding at each step.

**Routes to.** `pdf-create-change-request`, `pdf-decide-escalation`, `pdf-relationship-health`, `pdf-weekly-status`.

---

## P4 — Over-specified or hard-to-source role

**Signature.** A demand for a single person combining rare skills that is impractical to fill as stated (the "everything" hire). The risk is committing to a hire that never lands while the need goes unmet.

**Clarify.**
- What is the actual scope of work the role must cover?
- Which requirements are must-have and which are nice-to-have?
- What does the existing resourcing pool realistically offer?

**Response shape.** Narrow to must-haves and state the lead time honestly. Offer alternatives: split the requirement across two people, propose a flexible rotation by skill, or augment a near-fit with training. If the client will not flex, take a clear go/no-go decision with the account owner and communicate the constraint plainly.

**Routes to.** `pdf-create-capacity-plan`, `pdf-create-ramp-plan`, `pdf-decide-escalation`.

---

## P5 — Out-of-contract request / incremental scope creep

**Signature.** A request, often small and reasonable-sounding, for work outside the agreed scope; or a steady accumulation of such requests. The risk is that many small uncosted "favours" sum to a material impact on timeline or cost.

**Clarify.**
- What exactly is being asked, and is it one-off or recurring?
- What would have to be descoped or deprioritised to absorb it?
- What is the cumulative weight of recent small changes?

**Response shape.** Treat it as a change even when small. Prepare assumptions, estimate, and a revised plan; discuss internally with the account owner before responding to the client. Material changes get approved in writing and reflected in the contract where needed. Keep a clear record of every change, including the small ones that go through a light path, so the cumulative picture stays visible.

**Routes to.** `pdf-create-change-request`, `pdf-create-change-order`, `pdf-update-raid`.

---

## P6 — General stakeholder dissatisfaction

**Signature.** A stakeholder is unhappy and it does not map cleanly to P1–P5: a cooling relationship, repeated friction, a poor satisfaction score with no single obvious cause. The risk is treating a symptom while the cause persists.

**Clarify.**
- What is the evidence of dissatisfaction (behaviours, not impressions)?
- Which trust dimension is weakest: credibility, reliability, candour, orientation, cadence, or alignment?
- Is this one stakeholder or a pattern across several?

**Response shape.** Run a relationship-health read to locate the weak dimension. Address the specific dimension directly (a missed-commitment pattern is fixed differently from a candour gap). Use a 1:1 to reset where the issue is personal. Validate any satisfaction signal by talking to the people behind it rather than guessing.

**Routes to.** `pdf-relationship-health`, `pdf-prep-1on1`, `pdf-debrief-call`, `pdf-write-stakeholder-update`.

---

## Using the patterns

- More than one pattern can apply at once (a late expectation gap often arrives with dissatisfaction). Match all that fit and merge their clarify-questions and routes.
- When nothing fits, use the P6 general spine and say so in the diagnostic.
- Always frame the problem in the affected stakeholder's terms before generating options. An unframed fix usually solves the wrong problem.
