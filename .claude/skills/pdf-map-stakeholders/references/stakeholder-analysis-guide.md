# Stakeholder analysis guide

The models behind `pdf-map-stakeholders`, the canonical elicitation questions, and the derivation rules. All models are public scholarship or open practice; none is vendor-specific.

## Provenance

- **Influence / Interest grid.** The power/interest matrix attributed to Aubrey Mendelow (1991) and developed by Colin Eden and Fran Ackermann (*Making Strategy*, 1998). Four quadrants, four postures.
- **Salience.** The stakeholder salience attributes (power, legitimacy, urgency) from Ronald Mitchell, Bradley Agle and Donna Wood, "Toward a Theory of Stakeholder Identification and Salience" (*Academy of Management Review*, 1997). The named eight types in that paper are cited below for context; the workflow derives a simple priority tier instead of leaning on the typology.
- **Onion / ring view.** Alistair Cockburn's stakeholder rings (innermost: those who act on the system; outer rings: those affected or interested).
- **Trust level.** Adapted from the trust equation in David Maister, Charles Green and Robert Galford, *The Trusted Advisor* (2000): trust grows with credibility, reliability and intimacy, and falls as self-orientation rises.

## The Influence / Interest grid

Two axes, each high or low.

- **Influence.** The stakeholder's power over decisions this engagement depends on: budget, scope, resourcing, timeline, go/no-go, escalation outcomes.
- **Interest.** How closely they follow and care about the engagement day to day.

| Quadrant | Position | Posture |
|----------|----------|---------|
| Manage Closely | High influence, high interest | Active partnership. Co-prepare key moments. Most of your relationship effort goes here. |
| Keep Satisfied | High influence, low interest | Keep them confident and free of surprises. Brief, high-signal, infrequent. They can stop the engagement but do not want detail. |
| Keep Informed | Low influence, high interest | Give regular visibility and invite input at the right points. Often the people who do the work or feel the impact. |
| Monitor | Low influence, low interest | Light touch. Watch for movement into another quadrant as the engagement evolves. |

**Canonical questions.**

- *"How much influence does this person have over budget, scope, resourcing, or timeline decisions? (high / low)"*
- *"How much interest do they take in this engagement day to day? (high / low)"*

A stakeholder's quadrant is not permanent. Re-position on the `update` intent when influence or interest shifts (a reorganisation, a new sponsor, a change of priorities), and record the reason.

## Salience

Three yes/no attributes per stakeholder.

- **Power.** Can they impose their will (resources, authority, or coercive/normative leverage)?
- **Legitimacy.** Is their claim on the engagement appropriate and recognised (contractual, role-based, or moral)?
- **Urgency.** Is their claim time-sensitive and important to them right now?

**Derivation rule (priority tier).**

| Attributes present | Priority tier | Handling |
|--------------------|---------------|----------|
| All three (power + legitimacy + urgency) | **Definitive** | Top priority. Acts and reacts fast; manage closely and proactively. |
| Two of three | **Expectant** | Material. The missing attribute tells you the failure mode (e.g. power + urgency without legitimacy is a stakeholder to handle with care). |
| One of three | **Latent** | Low salience today. Track in case a second attribute appears. |
| None | **Non-salient** | Not a stakeholder for this engagement; record and move on. |

Derive the tier from the three answers. Never ask the user to name the tier directly.

**Context (the named eight types).** Mitchell, Agle and Wood label the combinations Dormant, Discretionary, Demanding, Dominant, Dangerous, Dependent, Definitive and Non-stakeholder. The workflow does not require these labels; the three-attribute derivation above is sufficient and clearer for delivery use. The names are recorded here only so a reader who knows the source paper can map across.

## Onion / ring view (optional)

When a stakeholder landscape is large, sort stakeholders into rings: those who act on or operate the delivery (innermost), those whose work is directly affected (middle), those with an interest or oversight but no direct involvement (outer). The ring view is a sense-making aid; the grid and salience drive the management plan.

## Management profile fields

For each material stakeholder (Manage Closely, Keep Satisfied, and any the user names), capture:

- **Motivations.** *"What does success on this engagement look like for them personally, and for their part of the organisation?"* Cover career goals, accountability, and the pressures they sit under.
- **Judged on.** *"What measures are they assessed against by their own leadership?"* Knowing their metrics lets you frame updates in their terms.
- **Working style.** *"How do they like to receive information: headline-first or detail-first, synchronous or written, frequent or sparse, formal or informal?"* Record a description. Do not reduce a person to a typology label.
- **Trust level.** Choose one (rubric below) with a one-line evidence note.
- **Engagement approach.** *"What is the one thing to get right with this person?"*

## Trust-level rubric

| Level | Signature |
|-------|-----------|
| **Nascent** | New or transactional. They rely on you for specific tasks; little shared context; benefit of the doubt not yet earned. |
| **Working** | Reliable two-way working relationship. Bad news can be shared; commitments are believed; some shared context. |
| **Trusted-advisor** | They seek your view on decisions beyond the contracted scope and act on it. Candour flows both ways. Low self-orientation is evident: they believe you act in their interest. |

Trust level rises through demonstrated credibility, kept commitments, and candour over time, and falls when you are seen to act mainly in your own interest. The level is an honest read, not an aspiration.

## House rules

- The map cites the register by ID; it never restates names, roles, or contact details as new facts.
- Inferred values from a dump are marked `[?]` until confirmed.
- British English. Every bullet and sentence ends with a period.
